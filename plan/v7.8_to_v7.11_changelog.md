## 1. Market Structure Changes

### 1.1 Removal of Heuristic Bots (16-agent to 8-agent)

| Parameter | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| `companies.n_bot_agents` | 8 | **0** |
| Total participants | 16 (8 learned + 8 bots) | **8 (pure MARL)** |

**Files**: `configs/default.yaml` line 128

**Justification**: The thesis investigates whether MARL agents can autonomously learn rational bidding and investment strategies in an ETS setting. A pure MARL configuration isolates the emergent behavior of learning agents without the confound of heuristic anchors. When bots are present, learned agents can free-ride on bot-generated price signals rather than discovering pricing fundamentals independently. The 8-agent pure MARL setup tests the strongest form of the research hypothesis: that PPO/HAPPO agents can develop market-rational behavior from scratch through self-play.

**EU ETS alignment**: The real EU ETS has no "heuristic" participants. All bidders are strategic actors (utilities, industrials, financial intermediaries) that learn and adapt. A pure learning-agent setup better reflects this reality than mixing hand-coded rules with learned policies.

---

### 1.2 Structural Over-Subscription (cap_overhead_pct: +2% to -10%)

| Parameter | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| `ets.cap_overhead_pct` | +0.02 (+2% surplus) | **-0.10 (-10% deficit)** |

**Files**: `configs/default.yaml` line 66

**Justification**: In v7.8.0 the emission cap was set 2% above total initial emissions, meaning the market started with a small surplus of allowances. This created weak price signals because total supply exceeded total demand. In v7.11.0 the cap is set 10% below total initial emissions, guaranteeing structural over-subscription: agents collectively need more allowances than are available. This forces meaningful auction competition and ensures the carbon price reflects genuine scarcity.

**EU ETS alignment**: The EU ETS Phase IV cap (starting 2021) was explicitly designed to create scarcity. The Linear Reduction Factor (LRF) of 4.3% p.a. post-2024 (Fit for 55 package) ensures the cap declines faster than natural emissions reductions, maintaining over-subscription. A -10% initial deficit is a stylized but directionally correct representation of the Phase IV scarcity regime.

### 1.3 Reserve Price / Price Floor Increase (30 to 45 EUR/t)

| Parameter | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| `auction.reserve_price` | 30.0 | **45.0** |
| `auction.price_min` | 30.0 | **45.0** |
| `secondary.sec_price_min` | 30.0 | **45.0** |

**Files**: `configs/default.yaml` lines 87, 162, 210

**Justification**: The reserve price acts as the auction floor. At 30 EUR/t (v7.8.0) the floor was well below the marginal abatement cost (MAC) of 48 EUR/t for coal-to-gas switching. This allowed agents to bid at non-economic prices (below fuel-switching cost), which is irrational in practice. Setting the floor at 45 EUR/t (just below MAC) ensures that any winning bid is at least marginally consistent with fundamental abatement economics. The comment in v7.11.0 config states: "just below MAC (48 EUR/t), prevents degenerate floor equilibrium."

**EU ETS alignment**: The EU ETS uses auction reserve prices set by the European Commission. While the real reserve price mechanism is complex (based on 2-month average of secondary market prices), the principle is that the reserve prevents auctions from clearing at trivially low prices during temporary demand slumps. The EU Market Stability Reserve (MSR) and the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) further support a de facto price floor. The European Commission's Impact Assessment for Fit for 55 assumed carbon prices of 45-60 EUR/t in the near term, making 45 EUR/t a realistic lower bound.

### 1.4 Quantity Multiplier Range Widened *(Reviewer-Addressed)*

| Parameter | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 (initial) | v7.11.0 (post-review) |
|-----------|--------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| `auction.qty_mult_low` | 0.3 | 0.85 | **0.5** |
| `auction.qty_mult_high` | 2.0 | 1.5 | **2.0** |

**Files**: `configs/default.yaml` lines 166-167

**Reviewer feedback**: The initial `[0.85, 1.5]` range was too restrictive. The floor conflated total procurement with auction-only demand and eliminated secondary market strategies. The ceiling prevented legitimate strategic over-bidding when agents anticipate thin secondary market liquidity.

**Post-review justification**: The wider `[0.5, 2.0]` range preserves strategic flexibility: agents can under-bid at auction to procure cheaply on secondary, or over-bid to front-load in thin markets. Structural scarcity is maintained by `cap_overhead_pct: -0.10` (cap 10% below emissions), which ensures aggregate demand exceeds supply regardless of individual bid multipliers.

### 1.5 Unsold Volume Rescheduled (Not MSR) *(Reviewer-Addressed)*

| Parameter | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 (initial) | v7.11.0 (post-review) |
|-----------|--------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| `ets.unsold_to_msr` | false | true | **false** |

**Files**: `configs/default.yaml` line 89

**Reviewer feedback**: Routing all unsold volume to MSR misreads the mechanism. Under Article 10(6) of the EU ETS Directive, unsold lots at auction are rescheduled to subsequent auctions. Only TNAC-triggered surpluses above the 833M threshold flow to the MSR. The `unsold_to_msr: true` setting over-inflated the reserve and tightened supply beyond what the regulation intends.

**Post-review justification**: `unsold_to_msr: false` — unsold lots roll over to the next year's auction supply via `rollover_unsold()`. The MSR continues to absorb supply through its normal TNAC-based withholding mechanism in `_apply_msr()`, which correctly implements the three-band withholding logic (Decision 2015/1814).

### 1.7 Bank Seed Range Increased

| Parameter | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| `stochastic.bank_seed_min` | 0.05 | **0.15** |
| `stochastic.bank_seed_max` | 0.15 | **0.35** |
| `ets.initial_bank_fraction` | 0.10 | **0.25** |

**Files**: `configs/default.yaml` lines 65, 458-459

**Justification**: Each episode starts agents with a random bank of allowances (carry-over from a hypothetical prior compliance period). The Total Number of Allowances in Circulation (TNAC) was approximately 1.5 billion allowances in 2021, compared to annual emissions of ~1.4 billion, representing ~107% of one year's need. A 25% bank seed is conservative relative to real-world carry-over.

## 2. Reward Function Changes

### 2.1 Reward Normalization: Per-Agent Budget to Fixed REWARD_SCALE

| Aspect | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Cost denominator | `max(company.annual_budget, 1.0)` (per-agent) | **1000.0 M EUR (fixed)** |
| Penalty denominator | `max(company.annual_budget, 1.0)` | **1000.0 M EUR** |
| Terminal value denominator | `max(company.annual_budget, 1.0)` | **1000.0 M EUR** |

**Files**: `src/environment/ets_environment.py` lines 2275, 2317-2318 (v7.11.0)

**Justification**: In v7.8.0 each agent's reward was normalized by its own annual budget, creating per-agent reward scales. Agent A1 (low budget) would have a different effective reward magnitude than Agent A6 (high budget) for the same absolute cost. This made HAPPO's sequential update unstable because the centralized critic saw wildly different reward scales from different agents. A fixed denominator (1000 M EUR) produces uniform reward scaling across all agents. The `RewardNormalizer` (EMA-based, in `ppo_agent.py`) handles remaining variance.

**Rational justification**: From an RL theory perspective, reward normalization should be consistent across agents in a shared-critic architecture. HAPPO's importance-weighted sequential update assumes comparable reward magnitudes. Per-agent normalization broke this assumption and required compensating adjustments (per-agent ESG scale, baseline subtraction) that added complexity without improving signal quality.

---

### 2.2 Removal of Baseline Cost Subtraction

| Aspect | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Baseline subtraction | `cost_norm -= estimated_need * clearing_price / budget` | **Removed** |

**Files**: `src/environment/ets_environment.py`

**Justification**: In v7.8.0 the cost signal subtracted an expected baseline cost (`estimated_need * clearing_price`) before normalization, making the reward relative to a hypothetical "buy at market" strategy. This had two problems: (1) the baseline was noisy because `clearing_price` varied episode to episode, and (2) it created a confusing signal where doing nothing yielded zero reward rather than negative reward. Removing it makes the cost signal absolute: spending money is always negative reward, which is a cleaner gradient for the optimizer.

---

### 2.3 Removal of Green Bonus and Efficiency Bonus (Shaping Rewards)

| Aspect | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 |
|--------|--------|---------|
| `green_bonus` | `beta_shaping * green_delta * fossil_scale * shaping_weight * (0.2 + w_green)` | **Removed** |
| `efficiency_bonus` | `1.5 * ef_improvement_ratio * time_weight * price_weight * shaping_weight` | **Removed** |
| Shaping reward channel | Present | **Always zero** |

**Files**: `src/environment/ets_environment.py`, reward channel keys reduced from 10 to 4

**Justification**: The v7.11.0 docstring (line 2266-2272) explains: "No shaping rewards (green_bonus, efficiency_bonus, gap_closure_credit removed). These created contradictory gradients and washed out the cost signal. Greening is already rational: lower emissions -> lower allowance need -> lower cost. ESG agents get an explicit ESG signal."

The fundamental insight is that green investment is already incentivized by the core cost signal: reducing emissions reduces the need to purchase allowances. Adding explicit green bonuses created double-counting: the agent was rewarded twice for the same action (once through lower compliance costs, once through the bonus). This makes the reward landscape non-stationary (the bonus decays but the cost savings don't) and confuses credit assignment.

**Rational justification**: In the real EU ETS, companies invest in decarbonization purely because it reduces their compliance costs (buying fewer allowances, lower penalty risk). There is no explicit "green bonus" in the market mechanism. Removing shaping bonuses makes the simulation's incentive structure match the real EU ETS more closely: the carbon price IS the green incentive.

---

### 2.4 Removal of Budget Penalty and Capex Penalty from Reward

| Aspect | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 |
|--------|--------|---------|
| `budget_penalty` | `company.compute_budget_penalty()` in reward | **Removed from reward** |
| `capex_penalty` | `company.compute_capex_penalty()` in reward | **Removed from reward** |
| Enforcement | Soft (penalty in reward) | **Hard (action clipping)** |

**Files**: `src/environment/ets_environment.py`

**Justification**: The v7.11.0 docstring (line 2271-2272) states: "Budget/capex penalties removed from reward. Enforced mechanically via investment_hard_gate and hard_cap_fraction action clipping. Spending is still recorded for diagnostics."

The problem with soft budget penalties was that they created death spirals: an agent that slightly overspent got a budget penalty, which counted as "spending" in the next budget check, triggering more penalty, etc. Hard enforcement (clipping actions so they cannot exceed the budget) is both simpler and more realistic: real companies have hard capital constraints, not soft ones.

---

### 2.5 Removal of Capex Penalty from Auction Reward

| Aspect | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Auction reward components | auction_cost + collateral + investment + opex_delta + mac + loan_interest + capex_penalty - baseline | **auction_cost + collateral + investment + opex_delta + mac** |
| Auction reward denominator | `max(company.annual_budget, 1.0)` | **1000.0 (REWARD_SCALE)** |
| Loan interest in auction reward | Included | **Excluded** (only in phase-2 reward via `record_spending`) |
| Baseline subtraction | `estimated_need * clearing_price / budget_divisor` | **Removed** |

**Files**: `src/environment/ets_environment.py` lines 1696-1736

**Justification**: The auction-phase reward is an intermediate signal for phase-1 decisions. It should only reflect costs directly attributable to auction+investment choices. Capex penalties and loan interest are computed later (compliance phase) and belong in the phase-2 reward. Including them in phase-1 created temporal leakage in credit assignment.

### 2.6 Terminal Bank Value: log1p Restored *(Reviewer-Addressed)*

| Aspect | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 (initial) | v7.11.0 (post-review) |
|--------|--------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| Formula | `log1p(capped_holdings / annual_need) * annual_need * terminal_price / budget_divisor` | `capped_holdings * terminal_price / REWARD_SCALE` | **`log1p(capped_holdings) * terminal_price / REWARD_SCALE`** |
| Cap | 2x annual need | 2x annual need | 2x annual need |

**Files**: `src/environment/ets_environment.py`

**Reviewer feedback**: The argument for linear was backwards — `log1p` correctly models diminishing marginal value of excess bank holdings, which is economically sound. The linear formula incentivizes pure hoarding up to the 2x cap, hurting secondary market activity.

**Post-review justification**: `log1p` restored. Diminishing returns ensure that the first unit of banked allowance is worth more than subsequent units, making it rational to sell excess holdings on the secondary market rather than hoarding. This aligns with real-world banking behavior where companies maintain compliance buffers but actively trade surpluses.

### 2.7 Terminal Queue Value Restored with Completion-Fraction Discount *(Reviewer-Addressed)*

| Aspect | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 (initial) | v7.11.0 (post-review) |
|--------|--------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| Terminal queue valuation | ESG-based NPV with gamma-discount | Removed (zeroed out) | **NPV of carbon savings × completion fraction** |
| Gaming prevention | None | N/A (removed) | **`value *= years_completed / total_delay`** |

**Files**: `src/environment/ets_environment.py`

**Reviewer feedback**: Without terminal queue value, agents won't initiate long-lead investments (e.g., 7-year offshore wind) past the episode midpoint. The gaming concern is valid but solvable with a completion-fraction discount — removing entirely is the wrong fix.

**Post-review implementation**: Terminal queue value restored with anti-gaming mechanism:
- Each queued project's value = `carbon_saved_per_year × terminal_price × annuity_factor × completion_frac`
- `completion_frac = years_completed / total_delay` (0.0 for just-started, 1.0 for nearly-complete)
- Carbon savings computed from switching avg fossil EF to green tech EF
- NPV discounted at `investment.discount_rate` (5%) over `terminal_payoff_years` (5 years)
- A 7-year offshore wind project started in the final year: `completion_frac = 0/7 = 0` → zero value
- Same project started in year 5: `completion_frac = 7/7 = 1.0` → full NPV credit

## 3. Exploration & Training Schedule Changes

### 3.1 Tabula Rasa Schedule Rebalancing

| Parameter | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 | Effect |
|-----------|--------|---------|--------|
| `epsilon_start` | 0.50 | **0.30** | Less random exploration initially |
| `epsilon_decay_frac` | 0.50 | **0.80** | Slower epsilon decay (explores longer) |
| `critic_warmup_frac` | 0.10 | **0.03** | Shorter critic-only warmup |
| `shaping_decay_frac` | 0.33 | **0.60** | Shaping decays more slowly |

**Files**: `configs/default.yaml` lines 25-31

**Justification**:

- **epsilon_start 0.50 -> 0.30**: 50% random actions is too disruptive for an 8-agent market. When half the agents take random actions simultaneously, the market dynamics become chaotic and the critic learns noise. 30% maintains exploration while preserving enough structure for meaningful price signals.

- **epsilon_decay_frac 0.50 -> 0.80**: Compensates for the lower start by maintaining exploration for a longer fraction of training. Total exploration is more evenly spread rather than front-loaded.

- **critic_warmup_frac 0.10 -> 0.03**: In v7.8.0, 10% of training (10k episodes at 100k) was spent with frozen actors. This wasted a substantial portion of the training budget on critic-only updates. 3% is sufficient for critic initialization, after which actor-critic co-training can begin.

- **shaping_decay_frac 0.33 -> 0.60**: In v7.8.0 shaping rewards (which have now been removed from the reward function anyway) decayed to zero by 33% of training. In v7.11.0 this value is kept for the `shaping_weight` variable used elsewhere, allowing a longer transition period.

## 4. Auction Mechanism Extension

### 4.1 Pay-As-Bid Pricing Rule Support

| Aspect | v7.8.0 | v7.11.0 |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Pricing rules | Uniform price only | **Uniform + pay-as-bid** |
| Config parameter | N/A | `auction.pricing_rule: "uniform"` |

**Files**: `src/auction/market_clearing_ets.py` lines 44-45, 198-207; `src/environment/ets_environment.py` lines 820, 1394

**Justification**: The v7.11.0 auction module adds support for pay-as-bid pricing, where each winner pays their own bid price rather than the marginal clearing price. The default remains "uniform" (EU ETS style), but the infrastructure allows comparative analysis.

**EU ETS alignment**: The EU ETS uses uniform pricing (all winners pay the clearing price, per Regulation 1031/2010). However, the UK ETS (post-Brexit) uses pay-as-bid pricing. Adding both options allows the simulation to compare these two real-world carbon market designs. This is directly relevant to the thesis's market design analysis: the effect of pricing rules on bidding strategy is a well-studied topic (Ausubel & Cramton, 2004; Hortacsu & McAdams, 2010).

**Implementation**: The `market_clearing_ets()` function now accepts an optional `pricing_rule` parameter. Under `"pay_as_bid"`, each agent's payment equals their own bid price times their allocated quantity (summed across bid tranches). Under `"uniform"` (default), behavior is unchanged from v7.8.0.

## 8. Summary of Rationale Themes

The changes from v7.8.0 to v7.11.0 follow five coherent design principles:

### Theme A: Simplify the Reward Signal
Remove auxiliary shaping rewards (green bonus, efficiency bonus, budget penalty, capex penalty, baseline subtraction) that created contradictory gradients. The carbon price mechanism itself is the green incentive -- duplicating it with bonuses washes out the cost signal. This follows the standard RL principle: simpler, sparser rewards produce more robust policies (Ng et al., 1999).

### Theme B: Enforce Constraints Mechanically, Not via Reward
Budget and capex limits are now enforced by clipping actions, not by adding penalties to the reward. This avoids death spirals and makes the constraint boundary sharp and predictable, which is easier for the agent to learn.

### Theme C: Align with Real EU ETS Mechanisms
- Unsold lots rescheduled per Art.10(6) *(post-review fix)*
- Reserve price near MAC (realistic floor)
- Structural over-subscription (Phase IV scarcity regime)
- Carry-forward: 1.0x cap, pending ablation *(post-review: needs evidence)*
- Realistic bank seed (prior-period carry-over)
- Pay-as-bid option (UK ETS comparison)

### Theme D: Stabilize Multi-Agent Training
- Fixed REWARD_SCALE across all agents (consistent critic targets)
- Lower initial exploration noise (30% vs 50%)
- Wide action bounds preserved (qty_mult 0.5-2.0) *(post-review fix)*
- Removal of per-agent budget normalization (eliminates cross-agent scale mismatch)

### Theme E: Pure MARL Evaluation
Remove heuristic bots to test whether learning agents can discover rational strategies through self-play alone, without hand-coded behavioral anchors. This is the strongest test of the MARL approach.
