# Suggested Changes to v7.11.0

## Diagnosis: Why the Run Failed to Produce a Functioning Market

The 70k-episode run produced three interrelated failures that share a single root cause.

### The Root Cause: Green Transition Killed Scarcity

At episode start, total emissions = 22.5 Mt and cap = 20.3 Mt (scarcity ratio 1.11x). The -10% `cap_overhead_pct` was designed to guarantee over-subscription. But the green agents (A5-A8, starting at 73-93% green) have near-zero emissions from the very first episode. Their combined need is ~2.9 Mt/yr. The brown agents (A1-A4) need ~17.6 Mt/yr but bid for only ~0.87x of their need (qty_mult_low constraint), so actual demand is ~15.3 Mt.

**Total auction demand (~10 Mt) is barely half of the cap (~20 Mt) in every year.**

The system reached a paradox: the -10% cap deficit was supposed to create scarcity, but it was calculated relative to INITIAL emissions. Once agents greened (even partially), their actual emissions dropped well below the cap. By the converged state, total emissions (~11 Mt) were below the cap until year 11 of 12.

### Failure Chain

```
Green agents have low emissions
  -> Low demand for allowances
    -> Auction under-subscribed
      -> Price = floor (45 EUR) every year
        -> No price signal for investment decisions
          -> Brown agents have no gradient to learn from
            -> Green fraction frozen at ~73% for 60k episodes
```

### Per-Agent Trap Analysis

| A1 | 22% | 5.28 Mt | ~2.53 Mt | 2.75 Mt | 381 M EUR (43% of budget) | ~8 years |

A1/A2 face a structurally impossible problem: 43% of their annual budget is consumed by penalties from year 0, AND the fastest green technology (solar, 2yr delay) takes ~8 years of max investment (20%/yr) to bring them from 22% to 100% green. They bleed penalties for 8 years before the investment pays off. The RL agent sees 8 years of negative reward before any positive signal — far beyond the effective horizon of gamma=0.99 discounting.

## Suggested Changes

### Change 1: Reduce Initial Heterogeneity (Priority: HIGH)

**Problem**: The 22%-93% green spread creates agents that are born into fundamentally different difficulty levels. A1's optimization landscape is almost entirely dominated by unavoidable penalties.

**Suggestion**: Narrow the initial mix spread so all agents start with similar emissions intensity.

```yaml
# v7.11.0 (current)
initial_mix:
  - [0.40, 0.40, 0.10, 0.05, 0.05]   # A1: 22% green, ef=0.528
  - [0.40, 0.40, 0.10, 0.05, 0.05]   # A2: 22% green
  - [0.15, 0.45, 0.20, 0.10, 0.10]   # A3: 43% green, ef=0.352
  - [0.15, 0.45, 0.20, 0.10, 0.10]   # A4: 43% green
  - [0.05, 0.25, 0.35, 0.20, 0.15]   # A5: 73% green, ef=0.177
  - [0.05, 0.25, 0.35, 0.20, 0.15]   # A6: 73% green
  - [0.00, 0.10, 0.30, 0.35, 0.25]   # A7: 93% green, ef=0.069
  - [0.00, 0.10, 0.30, 0.35, 0.25]   # A8: 93% green

# Suggested: tighter spread centered around ~50% green
initial_mix:
  - [0.25, 0.30, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10]   # A1: 45% green, ef=0.355
  - [0.20, 0.35, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10]   # A2: 45% green, ef=0.338
  - [0.15, 0.35, 0.25, 0.15, 0.10]   # A3: 50% green, ef=0.299
  - [0.15, 0.30, 0.25, 0.15, 0.15]   # A4: 55% green, ef=0.276
  - [0.10, 0.25, 0.30, 0.20, 0.15]   # A5: 65% green, ef=0.210
  - [0.10, 0.25, 0.25, 0.25, 0.15]   # A6: 65% green, ef=0.210
  - [0.05, 0.20, 0.30, 0.25, 0.20]   # A7: 75% green, ef=0.143
  - [0.05, 0.20, 0.30, 0.25, 0.20]   # A8: 75% green, ef=0.143
```

**EU ETS justification**: In the real EU ETS Phase IV, large power generators have already undergone substantial decarbonization. The EU electricity sector was approximately 44% renewable in 2023 (Eurostat). The extreme 22% green starting point (78% fossil) represents a pre-2010 generation fleet that no longer exists in the EU ETS participant pool. A 45-75% green range better reflects the actual heterogeneity among EU ETS Phase IV installations.

**Effect on scarcity**: With the suggested mixes, total initial emissions would be ~(0.355+0.338+0.299+0.276+0.210+0.210+0.143+0.143)*10 = ~19.7 Mt. With cap_overhead_pct=-10%, cap ~17.8 Mt. Scarcity ratio = 1.11x (same as now). But crucially, even after partial greening (to ~70% system-wide), emissions would still be ~12-13 Mt, which is above the cap in later years — maintaining scarcity throughout the episode.

### Change 2: Recalibrate cap_overhead_pct (Priority: HIGH)

**Problem**: -10% was calculated for the old initial mixes. With those mixes, the green agents had so few emissions that the cap was always over-sized relative to actual demand.

**Suggestion**: Either:

**(a) Adjust cap_overhead_pct to match the new mixes** (if Change 1 is adopted):

```yaml
# With tighter mixes and ~19.7 Mt initial emissions:
cap_overhead_pct: -0.10   # keeps cap at ~17.8 Mt, scarcity ratio ~1.11x
# This is fine IF mixes are tighter — because agents CAN'T green fast enough to
# collapse demand below cap within the first few years
```

**(b) Use dynamic cap calibration based on CONVERGED emissions, not initial**:

This is more complex but more robust. The cap should be calibrated so that even at 70-80% green (where agents converge), total emissions still exceed the early-year cap. One practical approach: increase the `lrf` (Linear Reduction Factor) so the cap drops faster, catching up with declining emissions.

```yaml
# Steeper cap reduction ensures scarcity persists even as agents green
lrf_phase1: 0.06    # was 0.043
lrf_phase2: 0.065   # was 0.044
```

**EU ETS justification**: The EU has already increased the LRF from 2.2% (Phase III) to 4.3-4.4% (Fit for 55). There is ongoing policy discussion about further increases to 5-6% post-2030 to align with the EU's 2040 climate target (90% reduction). A higher LRF is directionally consistent with future EU ETS evolution.

### Change 3: Lower the Price Floor (Priority: HIGH)

**Problem**: The 45 EUR/t floor prevents the auction from generating meaningful price variation. Since the market is under-subscribed, clearing price always hits 45 EUR. The agents learn that "price = 45 always" and have no incentive to bid strategically.

**Suggestion**: Lower the floor to allow price discovery.

```yaml
# Current
reserve_price: 45.0
price_min: 45.0
sec_price_min: 45.0

# Suggested
reserve_price: 20.0     # well below MAC, allows price discovery
price_min: 20.0
sec_price_min: 20.0
```

**Rationale**: The price floor should be a safety net, not the equilibrium. If the market is properly over-subscribed (via Changes 1-2), the clearing price will naturally settle above MAC (~48 EUR) without needing a high floor. A low floor (20 EUR) still prevents degenerate zero-price outcomes while allowing downward price movements that convey information (e.g., "demand is weak this year").

**EU ETS justification**: The EU ETS auction reserve price is set dynamically based on secondary market prices (typically 2-month average minus a margin). It is designed to be a floor, not a binding constraint. In 2023-2024, EU ETS prices ranged from 55-100 EUR/t, well above any floor. The mechanism only binds during price crashes (e.g., 2020 COVID). A 20 EUR floor captures this "emergency only" character.

**Alternative**: If the market structure changes (1-2) create proper scarcity, 30 EUR/t would also work. The key is: the floor should NOT be the expected clearing price.

---

### Change 4: Widen Quantity Multiplier Range (Priority: MEDIUM)

**Problem**: `qty_mult_low=0.85` constrains brown agents to bid for at least 85% of their estimated need. But their estimated need is based on current (high) emissions, which they can't afford to cover anyway. They "want" to bid less but can't — this limits strategic flexibility.

Meanwhile, `qty_mult_high=1.5` caps overbidding. Green agents that want to stockpile allowances for resale can't bid for more than 1.5x their (tiny) need.

**Suggestion**:

```yaml
# Current
qty_mult_low: 0.85
qty_mult_high: 1.5

# Suggested
qty_mult_low: 0.50      # allows strategic under-buying
qty_mult_high: 2.0       # allows strategic stockpiling
```

**Rationale**: Wider bounds give agents more strategic freedom. A brown agent might rationally bid for 50% of its need at auction and buy the rest on the secondary market (if secondary prices are lower). A green agent might bid for 2x its need to bank or resell allowances. These are both economically rational strategies.

**EU ETS justification**: In real EU ETS auctions there are no bid-quantity constraints beyond total supply. Bidders routinely submit demand for anywhere from a fraction of their compliance needs (expecting to buy more on secondary) to multiples of their needs (for banking/speculative purposes). Financial intermediaries bid purely for trading purposes with no compliance need at all.

### Change 5: Increase max_invest_frac (Priority: MEDIUM)

**Problem**: `max_invest_frac=0.20` limits each agent to shifting at most 20% of its energy mix per year. For A1 (22% green), reaching 100% green requires 4 investment cycles minimum. With solar's 2-year deploy delay, this means at least 8 years — most of the 12-year episode.

**Suggestion**:

```yaml
# Current
max_invest_frac: 0.20

# Suggested
max_invest_frac: 0.30     # faster transition possible
```

**Rationale**: At 30%, an agent starting at 45% green (with Change 1) could reach ~100% in 4-6 years, leaving several years to benefit from reduced compliance costs. This makes the green investment decision rational within the RL planning horizon.

**EU ETS justification**: Large EU utilities have made rapid portfolio shifts: Enel went from 47% to 65% renewable in 3 years (2020-2023). Orsted went from 70% fossil to 90% renewable in 5 years. A 30% annual mix shift is ambitious but within observed industry capabilities.

---

### Change 6: Reintroduce Heuristic Bots (Priority: MEDIUM)

**Problem**: Pure MARL creates a "coordination vacuum." When 8 learning agents simultaneously explore, the market has no stable pricing signal. All agents are noisy → price is noisy → critic learns noise → slow convergence. The 70k run showed that even after exploration decayed, agents couldn't escape the early-training equilibrium.

**Suggestion**: Reintroduce 2-4 bots as market anchors.

```yaml
# Current
n_bot_agents: 0

# Suggested
n_bot_agents: 4     # 8 learned + 4 bots = 12 total
```

**Rationale**: A small number of heuristic bots provides a "price backbone" — their fundamentals-based bidding (MAC -> penalty gradient) establishes a reasonable price level that learning agents can compete against. This is the standard approach in MARL financial market simulations (Vytelingum et al., 2008; Wellman et al., 2005).

**EU ETS justification**: The real EU ETS has a mix of sophisticated strategic bidders and more mechanical compliance-driven buyers. Industrial installations with small emissions often bid mechanically near their MAC. Adding bots represents this "price-taking" segment of the market.

**Thesis framing**: This doesn't weaken the MARL contribution. Frame it as: "learning agents compete in a market that includes both strategic and non-strategic participants, as in the real EU ETS." The research question becomes "can MARL agents learn to outperform heuristic strategies?" rather than "can MARL agents create a market from nothing?"

---

### Change 7: Scale Penalty with Inverse Green Fraction (Priority: LOW)

**Problem**: The penalty rate (138.75 EUR/t) hits all agents equally regardless of their emissions intensity. A1 (5.28 Mt emissions) pays 43% of budget as penalty. This is so large that the penalty signal dominates all other reward components, making the agent's optimization landscape essentially "minimize penalty" with no gradient for how.

**Suggestion**: This is more experimental, but consider scaling the effective penalty or providing a compliance-gap observation signal that normalizes shortfall relative to each agent's starting position.

**Alternative (simpler)**: Increase the carry_forward_cap back to 1.0x for the first few years, allowing brown agents to defer some penalty while investing. The idea: a "transition grace period."

```yaml
carry_forward_cap: 1.0    # back to v7.8.0 value, allows more deferral
```

**EU ETS justification**: The EU ETS provides free allocation to sectors at risk of carbon leakage (CBAM transition period, 2026-2034). This is explicitly a transition grace mechanism. Generous carry-forward serves a similar function in the simulation.

---

### Change 8: Shorter Episodes for Faster Iteration (Priority: LOW)

**Problem**: 12-year episodes with 2-7 year construction delays mean the reward for a green investment is delayed by multiple decision steps. This is a well-known challenge for RL (sparse, delayed reward).

**Suggestion**: Consider 8-year episodes during the exploration phase, then extend to 12 for final evaluation.

```yaml
n_years: 8    # shorter episodes for faster credit assignment
```

**Rationale**: 8 years is still long enough for solar (2yr delay) and onshore wind (4yr delay) to come online and produce benefits. Offshore wind (7yr delay) would be marginal, which matches reality (very few new offshore projects are commissioned for < 8yr horizons). Shorter episodes mean faster feedback loops and more episodes per wall-clock hour.

## Suggested Priority Order

| Priority | Change | Impact | Risk |
|----------|--------|--------|------|
| **P0** | Change 1 (Tighter initial mixes) + Change 2 (Recalibrate cap) | Fixes the scarcity collapse that kills price discovery | Low — config only |
| **P1** | Change 3 (Lower price floor) | Allows price variation when scarcity exists | Low — config only |
| **P2** | Change 4 (Wider qty range) + Change 5 (Faster investment) | Gives agents strategic freedom to adapt | Low — config only |
| **P3** | Change 6 (Reintroduce bots) | Provides price anchoring during exploration | Medium — design choice |
| **P4** | Change 7 (Carry-forward) + Change 8 (Shorter episodes) | Helps brown agents survive long enough to learn | Low — config only |

**Recommended approach**: Implement P0+P1 first. Run a 20k-episode smoke test. If the clearing price shows variation above the floor, proceed to P2. If still stuck, add P3 (bots).

---

## What NOT to Change

- **Reward function**: The v7.11.0 simplified reward (3 terms + terminal) is clean and correct. The problem is not the reward — it's the market structure that produces no price signal for the reward to relay.
- **Neural network architecture**: The actor-critic architecture is not the bottleneck.
- **HAPPO update**: The sequential update is appropriate for the cooperative-competitive structure.
- **ESG signal**: The saved-carbon-years formula is well-designed. Keep it.
- **Pay-as-bid option**: Good to have for comparison experiments but not needed for the primary run.
